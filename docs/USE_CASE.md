# Useful pilot and economic hypothesis

An evaluation lead comparing two versioned models on a predeclared benchmark may have slow, expensive judge/rubric tasks in important strata and quick results in easy strata. Shipping a complete-case score can quietly change the target benchmark. Waiting for everything may consume unnecessary evaluation capacity. EvalEnvelope makes the remaining uncertainty actionable: retain the named benchmark, ask whether any legal completion changes the threshold decision, and freeze the next affordable coverage-compliant scores that remove the most declared uncertainty.

The demo establishes a concrete failure cost mechanism (a completed-only pass conflicts with the eventual full benchmark failure), and the contrast establishes a collection-choice mechanism (under the same budget, 9/10 versus 1/10 interval-width reduction). These are synthetic deterministic results, not observed production cost savings. Cost units are supplied assumptions. Narrowing an interval may clarify either PASS or FAIL, and sometimes clarifies neither. If all items have the same range/weight and cost, shortest-cost-first is equally good. Coupled scoring or changing benchmarks require another model.

Potential buyers are evaluation/platform teams already running scorers; an open-source local certificate can integrate into a release review or run scheduler through JSON. A plausible paid service would be maintaining exporter adapters, review integrations, reproducible score-import pipelines and support for teams' declared constraints. Willingness to pay, actual users, revenue and realized savings are all **unknown**. There is no hosted service or paid plan in this repository, and no pricing assertion. A pilot should measure whether the bounds change real review/collection choices, how often they remain inconclusive and the work required to bind trustworthy identities. Failed pilots are valid evidence to revise the scope.
